Hamiltonian may change in time & vary according to some params. In this case, system evolves according to Schrödinger’s equation with time-dependent Hamiltonian: $$H(t)|ψ(t)⟩=iℏddt|ψ(t)⟩$$Evolution of the system is much $>$ complicated with time-dependent Hamiltonian. In this case, the corresponding unitary transformation is given by $$U(t, t_0)=\mathcal{T}\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{i}{\hbar}\int_{t_0}^t dt'\,H(t')\right)$$ where $\mathcal{T}$ is the time-ordering operator. Simple exponential (without $\mathcal{T}$) is exact only when $$[H(t), H(t')]=0$$ for all $t, t'$; in the general case, non-commutativity at different times requires time-ordering (or Dyson series expansion), which is the basis for the Trotter approximation.

[[Quantum adiabatic theorem]] explains the behaviour of the system when the Hamiltonian changes sufficiently slowly.
